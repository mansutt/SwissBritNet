Bound to Basel

The linguist and theologian Johann Jacob Frey, born in Basel in 1606, fell in love with Britain in his early twenties and at age 29 he wrote: the very secrets of my heart […] are so deeply engaged for England that I hope one day to end my life in it.

Brought up as the only son of a solicitous, early-widowed mother, Frey displayed brilliant intellectual gifts from his teens. Having graduated MA from Basel at age 21, he went to study in Geneva, Lyons, and finally Oxford, where he signed the Bodleian Library’s user book and became a member of Christ Church College in 1629. He was also ordained in the Anglican Church in May 1630. This means that at age 24, he must have spoken English well enough to preach, and indeed he also corresponded in excellent English with women (who did not usually know Latin) and with scholars who normally used Latin for written communication.

From January 1629, Frey was tutor to Richard, Viscount Dungarvan, the oldest son of the powerful Earl of Cork. In the summer of 1633, he toured France with young Dungarvan and then went back to Britain, doing research for the scholarly Irish archbishop James Ussher, for example collecting manuscripts on the Protestant community of the Waldenses.

In the summer of 1635, after Dungarvan’s marriage, Frey returned to Basel. He had been offered a professorship in Greek, which he delayed taking up for 18 months because he felt "bound to Basel against my will" but finally accepted and gave his inaugural lecture on August 18, 1635. In the autumn of that year, Frey turned down a pressing offer to tutor the young sons of the Duke of Buckingham; the boys were then brought up at the court of Charles I.

Frey’s wish to return to Britain for good in the service of the Church came close to fulfillment when he was offered the Deanery of Armagh in Northern Ireland. To be able to claim his estate from his mother (who wanted him in Basel), he had to get married and indeed found "a young gentlewoman" who was willing to follow him. He was planning to move there in the spring of 1637 but died of the plague in August 1636, just 30 years old, on the very day when the "Rat" of Basel had decided to let him go.

Frey was intensely mourned by Swiss and English friends as a brilliantly gifted and lovable man. As late as 1653, Archbishop James Ussher fondly remembered the exchanges he enjoyed when our Frey was among the living. The few hundred letters of his English correspondence are a unique corpus among the ca. 50,000 17th- and 18th-century letters which are kept in the University Library of Basel and bear witness to an intense personal and intellectual connection.

Regula Hohl Trillini
